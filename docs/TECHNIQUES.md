# The author's techniques (register, literature check and paper plan)

Who made what. The physics, fluid and optics techniques of this engine are the work of **Agustin Diaz-Cano** (Wylan Studios), written first as single-file HTML demos in `game-engine/` (not in git) and then ported into the game. This file records them under the names he gave them, says what each one is used for, where it lives in the code, and which known technique it sits next to. It is the base for the paper he plans to write.

**What this file claims, after the literature check of 2026-10-23.** Authorship is recorded as the author states it in his own demos (the labels "Diaz-Cano" in the HTML comments). Two AI-assisted literature reviews were run (called review 1 and review 2 below). They are not peer reviewed and no patent search was done. Their result is the same on the main point: **most pieces are re-parameterisations or combinations of known techniques**, and only technique 2 came out as "possibly new" (and only in review 2, with medium confidence). What is the author's own is the particular formulas, constants and the way they are combined to be cheap and good-looking. Wording rule for public text: write "the author's formula" and "re-parameterisation of X"; do not write "invented" or "novel" for anything.

Rule for every session: keep the author's names for his techniques in docs and comments, never present them as standard, and name the known technique next to them.

**Claims from the reviews that are still only claims (to measure, not to publish as fact):**

- Technique 1 widens the reflection curve and, in review 2's words, "breaks energy conservation" at 45 degrees. Check with a white-furnace test before saying it either way.
- Techniques 1 and 3 are **not** cheaper than the standard: `x^5` is three multiplications, while a non-integer power is `exp2(log2(x) * p)` on the special-function unit. Claim the look and the control, then measure; do not claim speed.
- Technique 2 "fails the white-furnace test" (review 2). Same: measure.
- Review 2 says technique 4 costs a third of Gerstner because it only moves `y`. Not checked.

### 7.3 Technique 8 and a ship pulled by gravity

As a game rule, the Le Sage "shadow" model reduces to an attractive force that stays finite at zero distance. The 0.25 "saturation" is the same device as the softening length of n-body codes (Plummer 1911), so any softened gravity gives the same behaviour. The theory is refuted as physics (section 3, row 8), which does not matter for a game rule as long as it is not presented as physics.

What a ship pulled by planets needs is the force law plus an integrator:

- Force on a body from each source: `a = G * m * d / (|d|^2 + eps^2)^(3/2)` (Plummer); `eps` is the author's 0.25 or a per-planet value.
- Fixed 1/60 s step with leapfrog (velocity Verlet), which keeps orbital energy bounded, and a seeded sim, so runs stay replayable.
- Planets on rails (a stored orbit or a Kepler formula) so they do not drift; the ship, shots and debris are the bodies that feel the pull.
- A trajectory preview is the same function integrated forward N steps.
- The sim is already 2D on the ground plane (x, z), so a top-down space map fits without a 3D rewrite.
