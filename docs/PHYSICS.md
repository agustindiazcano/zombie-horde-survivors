# Physics

The single place that explains how things move, collide and fall in the game. There is **no physics engine in the game itself**: everything is hand-written kinematics inside `src/sim` (pure code, no Three.js, no DOM). The one exception is the Map 12 lab, which runs Rapier (section 6) as a separate module that nothing else uses yet; the user's own sphere solver (section 5) is kept as the reference for a later Rust/WASM port. Keep this file in sync whenever a physics rule changes, and put new formulas here before or together with the code.

## 1. Ground rules

| Rule             | Detail                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fixed step       | 60 Hz (`FIXED_DT = 1/60`, `src/sim/constants.ts`). The render interpolates with `prev` fields and `alpha`.                                                       |
| Deterministic    | Randomness comes from a seeded RNG (`src/sim/rng.ts`), so a run can be replayed.                                                                                 |
| Plane and height | The world is 2D on the ground plane (`x`, `z`). Height `y` exists only for jumps, climbing, grenades, swimming depth and aircraft altitude; it is not a 3D body. |
| Units            | Metres and seconds. Speeds in m/s, accelerations in m/s², angles in radians.                                                                                     |
| Data-driven      | Every number below lives in `data/game.json` (or `data/items.json`) and is validated by the zod schema in `src/sim/config.ts`.                                   |

## 2. What we have

**One gravity.** `gravity` in `data/game.json` (20 m/s²) is copied into `GameState.gravity` when a game starts. Jumps, falls off a box, the climb speed, grenade arcs (and their aiming preview) and the Map 12 lab all read `state.gravity`; the lab's Gravedad slider writes it, so it changes the player's jump and fall too. It is a live setting of the lab, not a recorded input: a replay of a run in which the slider moved would not match. Cosmetic particles (`gore.ts`, `looseParts.ts`, 22) keep their own constant. The `PhysicsParams.gravity` default (24) is only used by the headless scripts.

## 3. What we do not have

| Missing                         | Consequence today                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Forces, mass, impulses          | Nothing is pushed by a force; shoves are fixed distances                                                                    |
| Inertia for walking units       | They stop and turn instantly (only vehicles ramp speed with `accel`)                                                        |
| Friction                        | No sliding, no slippery or sticky surfaces                                                                                  |
| Projectiles with ballistics     | Bullets are instant rays: no travel time, bullet drop or drag                                                               |
| Rigid bodies, rotation, ragdoll | Corpses and gibs are visual only; the Map 12 lab has spinning balls, boxes and ragdolls (Rapier) but no enemy uses them yet |
| Slopes affecting speed          | The terrain height is read, but it does not slow or accelerate units                                                        |
| A physics engine in the game    | Rapier runs only in the Map 12 lab; live units stay on the 2D collision and nothing in the lab moves them yet               |

## 5. The own sphere solver (reference; the Map 12 lab no longer uses it)

Source: `game-engine/ai_studio_code (1).html` (the user's prototype). Code: `src/sim/physics/world.ts`, `ragdoll.ts`, `scenarios.ts` (pure, deterministic; still measured by `npm run bench:physics`; kept for the Rust/WASM port, phases 6 to 8 of the plan). Plan and results: [PLAN-physics-lab.md](PLAN-physics-lab.md), [worklog](worklog/2026-10-21-physics-lab-map-12.md).

Each fixed step (1/60 s) runs `substeps` (3) sub-steps of length `h`:

1. **Predict:** `v += g*h` (gravity 24 m/s²), `v *= 1 - airDrag*h`, `x += v*h`; the old position is kept.
2. **Pairs:** a spatial hash (cell 2.6 m, 16384 buckets, 27 neighbours) lists the spheres within touching range; a pair that closes faster than `restSpeed` (1.5 m/s) owes a bounce of `restitution * closing speed`, with `restitution = 1 - 0.273` (the silk viscosity).
3. **`iterations` (2) passes of:** bone constraints (mass weighted, `stiffness` per bone); sphere contacts, pushed apart by `overlap / (w_a + w_b + 1/(k*h²))` where `w = 1/mass` and `k = kHooke` (100 000 N/m: Hooke's `F = k * overlap` as the compliance of the constraint); Coulomb friction on the displacement since the sub-step began (it cancels at most `friction * push`, `friction` 0.8); the same for the ground (with its slope from the height grid), boxes (props with a collider) and the arena walls.
4. **Speed:** `v = (x - x_old) / h`; then the owed bounces, a damping of the speed along each bone (`boneDamping` 0.3), and bodies that touched something and move under `sleepSpeed` (0.3 m/s) are stopped.

Blast: `impulse = blastPower * (1 - d/R) / (d + 0.25)`, away from the centre with a lift of 18 m/s times the falloff; the velocity change is `impulse / mass`. The 0.25 (the saturation) keeps it finite at `d = 0`. `explosion` events of the sim (grenades) trigger it in the lab.

| What the prototype did                                          | What the lab does                                                                                   | Why                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Math.random`, variable `dt` (up to 0.05)                       | seeded RNG, fixed step                                                                              | `src/sim` must be deterministic; same run, same hash                                                                       |
| All masses equal in contacts (`* 0.5`)                          | mass-weighted separation and impulses                                                               | a heavy body must move a light one more than the reverse                                                                   |
| Separation by position plus a velocity impulse and a Hooke push | everything in position (small steps), Hooke as the contact compliance, friction on the displacement | measured: the velocity version let a 91-ball pyramid slide apart (top ball fell 2.7 m in 10 s); this one holds it (2.6 cm) |
| Hooke push only on approach (`vn < 0`)                          | the contact constraint acts whenever they overlap                                                   | a resting pile needs support force at rest                                                                                 |
| Position correction and a spring force on the same bone         | position correction only, plus damping along the bone                                               | both together on one stretch oscillate                                                                                     |
| One hash cell for every size                                    | `addBody` refuses a sphere wider than the cell                                                      | a wider body is missed by the 27-cell search without any warning                                                           |
| "Boxes" were spheres drawn as cubes with a fake spin            | boxes are not simulated                                                                             | a sphere cannot stack like a box; oriented boxes need Rapier (Phase B6)                                                    |

The ragdoll (`ragdoll.ts`, 11 joints, 13 bones) is driven onto the marine zombie model (Mixamo) by `src/render/ragdollRig.ts`: see the worklog. The plain Quaternius zombie ragdoll was dropped on 2026-10-21 (deprecated by the user): only marine zombies have one. Numbers to retune live in the Map 12 panel before they are copied anywhere.

## 6. The Map 12 lab on Rapier

Code: `src/sim/physics/rapierWorld.ts` (`RapierWorld`) and `bodies.ts` (shared constants and types, no Rapier import so the main bundle stays light), drawn by `src/render/physicsLabView.ts`, driven by `src/physicsLab.ts`. Package: `@dimforge/rapier3d-deterministic-compat` 0.21.0, loaded as its own chunk (4.4 MB, 1.7 MB gzip) the first time Map 12 opens.

| Rule            | Detail                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Determinism     | One `world.step()` per game step at `timestep` 1/60, bodies created in call order, seeded RNG only. `npm run check:rapier` and `npm run bench:physics` print a state hash (positions and rotations as float bits) that must repeat. Checked in Node 24 only, not in a browser. |
| Scenery         | A heightfield from `terrain.heights` (checked against `heightAt`: 0.0000 m worst difference), one fixed cuboid per prop box that is not water and does not let shots pass (from 2 m under the ground to its `height`), four arena walls 60 m tall.                             |
| Balls and boxes | Free bodies with rotation and CCD. Defaults (guesses): ball radius 0.5 m, 6 kg; box half side 0.5 m, 25 kg; friction 0.8, bounce 0.3, damping 0.05 linear and 0.6 angular.                                                                                                     |
| Ragdoll         | Ten capsule bodies (torso, head, two arms in two parts, two legs in two parts), 80 kg at scale 1, joined by spherical joints, with hinge joints (limits 0 to 2.5 rad, one way only) at the elbows and knees. Bones of one ragdoll never collide with each other (a hook).      |
| Joint positions | `ragdollNodes` gives the eleven joints in the `NODE` order from the capsule ends; `RagdollRig.apply` poses the model's bones from them (same rig as before).                                                                                                                   |
| Solver          | 16 iterations (8 left the ragdoll sliding for 15 s, 12 still moved 0.35 m in 2 s, 16 and 20 sleep).                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Blast           | Speed change `min(blastPower * (1 - d/R) / (d + 0.25) / 10, 60)` m/s away from the centre plus `18 * (1 - d/R)` upward, the same for every body whatever its mass (applied as impulse times mass).                                                                             |
| Gravity         | The game's one `GameState.gravity` is written into Rapier every step.                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Not yet         | Live units do not exist in Rapier (the 2D collision stays), so nothing pushes a crate but a blast. Spherical joints have no limits (hips and shoulders can twist too far). Shots do not push bodies yet.                                                                       |
